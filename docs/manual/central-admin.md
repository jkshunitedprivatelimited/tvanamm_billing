# Central admin guide

Manage the business across outlets, publish standards and oversee supply operations. Use outlet and date filters before comparing results.

[Back to all guides](README.md)

## Onboard a franchise and activate its outlet

**Where:** All outlets

Invite the owner and review the outlet details they provide.

1. Create an invitation with the requested franchise and owner information.
2. Share the complete invitation link through your normal communication channel.
3. Use Refresh progress to check acceptance and submitted outlet details.
4. Open Review details when the setup is ready for review.
5. Check the address, contact information and transport charge, then use Approve & activate.
6. Complete fresh OTP verification if prompted, then continue the approval.
7. Open the activated outlet to complete menu, employee and device setup.

Owner verified means the invitation identity was verified; it does not itself create an active outlet. Avoid creating a duplicate branch while an existing setup is awaiting review.

## Manage an existing outlet

**Where:** All outlets → Outlet → Billing & team

Review an outlet’s sales, spending, staff and devices.

1. Choose the outlet by name.
2. Use Bills & refunds to inspect bills and permitted refund actions.
3. Use Expenses to review employee spending and reasons.
4. Use Attendance to review staff presence and shifts.
5. Use Team & devices to maintain employee access and registered billing devices.
6. Use the outlet’s Reports and Stock links for detailed investigation.

Use Add branch for a branch you are creating directly, selecting the correct ownership and franchise. Check whether the branch already exists first.

## Publish the menu

**Where:** Menu

Maintain menu standards and deliver changes to billing.

1. Choose the master or outlet scope before editing.
2. Add or update categories, items, prices and the other available menu options.
3. Review the changes and save them.
4. Publish to the intended outlets and check any price-overwrite choices.
5. Confirm that the outlet billing menu received the publication.

Draft edits are not live until published. Outlet-specific edits can differ from the master. Earlier bills keep their recorded item and price history.

## Monitor business performance

**Where:** Business overview → Reports

Find sales changes, low stock and outlets needing attention.

1. Choose the time period and outlet scope.
2. Review business totals and the outlet comparison where available.
3. Open sales, payment or stock details for an outlet needing attention.
4. Use Outlet health in Reports to compare current and previous sales and review readiness.
5. Check Activity history when you need to investigate a recorded business or account change.

Business overview is for current decisions; activity history records changes. No recorded sales does not by itself mean a disconnected device or a closed store.

## Choose the correct central report

**Where:** Reports

Compare outlets and investigate money, staffing and supply costs.

1. Use Sales overview for gross and net sales, cash, UPI, discounts and refunds.
2. Use Outlet health for outlet comparisons and Top-selling items or Menu performance for item demand.
3. Use Team and Attendance where shown for employee sales and working time.
4. Use Cash reconciliation for drawer differences, Expenses for spending, and Discounts & refunds where shown for adjustments.
5. Use Stock & wastage for movements and losses; Supply orders for outlet replenishment; Purchases & suppliers for procurement.
6. Choose the period and scope, then download the appropriate CSV from the report or Exports & records.

Revenue, expense totals and stock valuation are different measures. Net sales is not a complete profit calculation. Unsynced sales and incomplete stock records limit what a report can show.

## Set up the supply catalogue

**Where:** Supply operations → Items & prices

Define the stock items outlets can order or record.

1. Create or review each item with its name, unit and available item settings.
2. Use consistent base units, such as millilitres for milk and grams for sugar.
3. Set purchasing rules: milk and sugar may be bought locally; tea powder must remain company-supplied.
4. Check ordering packs and prices before making the item available.
5. Confirm the item appears in the intended outlet stock or ordering screen.

Do not enter made-up opening balances. Batch-tracked goods need batch information and cannot use the employee quick-entry form.

## Manage warehouses and suppliers

**Where:** Supply operations → Warehouses / Suppliers

Maintain where supplies are held and who provides them.

1. Create warehouse records with the correct code and name.
2. Create supplier records with the required details.
3. Review and approve suppliers using the available controls.
4. Check the correct warehouse and supplier whenever creating a purchase.

Use real business records. These are supply-management screens, not franchise-owner daily purchase screens.

## Purchase stock for the warehouse

**Where:** Supply operations → Purchases

Record procurement and the goods actually received.

1. Create a purchase order for the intended supplier and warehouse.
2. Add item quantities and review the purchase details.
3. Open the order and use its next available workflow action.
4. When the goods arrive, enter the supplier invoice and receipt details requested by the screen.
5. Record actual received quantities and any required batch details.
6. Check the updated order and warehouse stock.

An order is a request to buy; receipt records arrival. Do not record the same goods again as a separate inward entry.

## Fulfil outlet orders

**Where:** Supply operations → Outlet deliveries

Move paid outlet orders through approval, stock allocation and dispatch.

1. Open the order and check the outlet, quantities and payment status.
2. Select the warehouse and approve the order when it is eligible.
3. Allocate the stock using the action shown for that order.
4. Dispatch the allocated goods and review the resulting delivery information.
5. Monitor partial dispatches and the outlet’s receipt or discrepancy.

Dispatching goods does not mean the outlet received them. Partial deliveries remain distinguishable from complete deliveries.

## Set stock baselines and review losses

**Where:** Stock control → Outlet → Current stock & alerts / Counts & wastage

Establish real balances and investigate differences.

1. Enter measured current quantities if the outlet has an opening count.
2. Otherwise begin from the next confirmed receipt or local purchase.
3. Set minimum levels for alerts.
4. Review employee counts and their explanations before approving adjustments.
5. Review recorded wastage and returns, then investigate repeated losses.

Billing remains available without stock. A physical count is the remaining quantity; wastage is a specific loss. Do not deduct the same loss twice.

## Create and publish a recipe

**Where:** Supply operations → SOP standards / Recipes

Define ingredient usage per serving so sales can update stock consistently.

1. Read SOP standards and identify any missing yields, spoon or scoop weights, or serving sizes.
2. Measure and confirm missing quantities before publication.
3. In Recipes, create a menu-item, add-on or prepared-base recipe and choose the relevant billing link when available.
4. Prepare the recipe version and enter ingredient quantities, units, yield and the required serving information.
5. Validate the version and publish only when its measurements are complete.
6. Publish the corresponding menu so billing uses the intended recipe version.
7. Check stock update status after sales to verify consumption is being processed.

Published versions preserve history; prepare a new version for changes. SOP notes and AI drafts are not automatically live recipes. Unconfirmed measurements must not be guessed.

## Use JKSH AI and recipe drafts

**Where:** JKSH AI

Analyse business data and prepare editable SOP or recipe drafts.

1. Ask a focused question with the outlet and period you want to analyse.
2. For a recipe draft, provide the confirmed ingredients, measurements, yield and method you already have.
3. Review the generated draft and its missing-information notes.
4. Save the draft if useful, then reopen and edit it as needed.
5. Use the normal recipe review and publishing process once all required measurements are confirmed.

AI draft creation does not publish a menu, change inventory or confirm missing measurements. Check figures against reports before acting.

## Handle a stock recall

**Where:** Supply operations → Stock recalls

Identify and control a specific batch that should no longer be used.

1. Confirm the affected item and batch from the supply records.
2. Create the recall with a clear reason.
3. Review the trace results and affected locations.
4. Use the available recall controls and coordinate removal or return of affected goods.
5. Complete the recall only after the required follow-up is resolved.

A recall concerns an affected batch. Routine spills, spoilage and stock counts belong in the normal wastage and count workflows.

## Check billing-to-stock updates

**Where:** Supply operations → Billing sync

Resolve delayed or failed stock updates without re-entering sales.

1. Review pending updates, failures and the last check.
2. Use the available sync or retry action for eligible failed records.
3. Refresh to confirm that the records progressed.
4. If failures continue, check the linked recipe and raise the exact error with support.

Do not create another bill to force stock deduction. Automatic processing also needs the background services to be running; hosting the web pages alone does not start those services.

## Troubleshooting and support

**Where:** The screen where the issue occurred

Resolve common issues without duplicating records.

1. If OTP delivery fails, check the mobile number and connection, then use the offered retry after the countdown. Never send an OTP to support.
2. If a report is empty, check the outlet, dates and whether offline bills have synced.
3. If a save has an unclear result, refresh or check history before submitting another entry.
4. If stock is wrong, check receipts, units, recipe links, counts and wastage before adjusting it.
5. For an unresolved issue, record the screen name, time, outlet, record reference and exact error. Share a screenshot with private information hidden.

Use only the actions available to your role. An unavailable action can mean missing setup, missing access or a temporary connection problem; it does not mean you should create duplicate records.
